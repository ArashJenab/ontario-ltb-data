What Ontario's rental disputes actually cost, and who pays

Built only from two public sources: the Landlord and Tenant Board's own order export and Statistics Canada's census. No private or scraped data, no named parties, and findings reported in whichever direction they came out.

The Board handles a normal-sized caseload by international standards. What is not normal is where the weight of it lands. 9,291 people who own a single rental unit bring 37.7% of all landlord cases, and for 85.8% of them it happens once and never again. By the time an order arrives the typical one is owed 4.04 months of rent on their only property. On the other side, the evictions tenants most often actually experience barely appear in this record at all.

What this costs someone who owns one unit

The aggregate figure, roughly $123M at stake across the province, invites the reading that a class of landlords received a windfall. It is spread across about 12,000 separate landlords, and two thirds of them are people, not companies. Set out per landlord, the same money looks entirely different.

Who turns up, and who has help

Orders name who attended the hearing and who appeared for them, so both can be counted directly rather than inferred.

This one cuts cleanly against the landlord side of the ledger and belongs here for that reason. Whatever else is true about who bears the financial loss, the party facing loss of housing is the one more likely to be absent and far less likely to have anyone speaking for them.
